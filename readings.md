Michel Waisvisz, Manager or Musician? About Virtuosity in Live Electronic Music (2006) — argues you must play electronic systems, not merely operate them, and worries about interfaces that are too easy. Short, polemical, perfect opener. link

Laetitia Sonami, Requiem for the Lady's Glove (Sound American SA6) — why she abandoned her signature instrument after 20 years; mastery, wearables, and building for "attractive weaknesses." A well-known artist reflecting critically on her own work, which is the good version of that. writings · PDF


Luigi Russolo, The Art of Noises (1913) — the founding manifesto for building noise machines and why. Pairs beautifully with the piezo-box assignment.

John Cage, The Future of Music: Credo — noise as music, inventing new means. (audio culture)


Jaron Lanier, You Are Not a Gadget (the MIDI chapter) — "lock-in": how one rigid interface quietly narrowed what music could be. Musician/technologist, non-academic. PDF

Alexander Galloway, The Interface Effect — interface not as window but as its own zone with a politics. Denser; excerpt it. link
Don Norman, The Design of Everyday Things — affordances (your call).
Marshall McLuhan, Understanding Media — the deep background for "interface" as a concept (extensions of the body, medium-as-message).

George Lewis, Too Many Notes: Computers, Complexity and Culture in Voyager (2000) — the composer on his interactive improvising software; argues that cultural values get built into systems and interfaces. A great counterweight to the design-theory pieces, and it foregrounds race/agency. Published in Leonardo Music Journal but very readable. PDF


Nicolas Collins, intro to Handmade Electronic Music — on "intuitive and sensual experimentation" lost to prefab instruments, and getting past fear of the circuit. The why behind your whole course. link
intro


Tarek Atoui — interview with C. Spencer Yeh, "On Hospitality, Collaboration and Expanding Musical Instruments" (Frieze). The most essay-like published conversation on his instrument-building logic. link · deeper dive in the book The Reverse Sessions / The Reverse Collection (interviews by Lissoni & Wood). book

Senyawa — Tone Glow 052 (Joshua Minsoo Kim), a long, substantive interview covering the instrument-building and the decentralized-release philosophy. link · and a good critical companion essay, "Rearranging the World: Alkisah" (Glissando). link



Jaime Oliver La Rosa (Lima) is the headline. He builds custom gesture instruments — the Silent Drum and MANO, camera-tracked controllers running in Pure Data (he did his PhD with Miller Puckette), open-source, Guthman first prize 2009. And he wrote the essay: "Design and Appropriation in Open Source Computer Musical Instruments: A Case Study of the Silent Drum" (eContact! 18.3). Peruvian, artist-written, conceptual, custom instrument, and Pd under the hood — it hits every criterion you set, and ties straight to the course's tools. essay · instrument


Ale Hop / Alejandra Cárdenas (Peru, Berlin-based) — artist-researcher; founded Contingent Sounds and edited Border-Listening / Escucha-Liminal, decolonial listening from Latin America. The writing counterpart to Oliver's building. interview · site
